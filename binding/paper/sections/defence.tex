\section{Restoring Binding Soundness}\label{sec:defence}

\S\ref{sec:results} rules out two easy fixes: re-verifying the same proof at
the origin keeps every \GA hit and B1 (\Abl{3}), and RFC~3986 normalisation in
front of a policy closes only dot segments. We evaluate defences that bind the
check to the \emph{executor's} view.
\emph{D-A} (\texttt{origin-routed}) applies the standard remedy for
normalisation bypasses---authorise the representation the router acts
on~\cite{p2:spring2016_5007,p2:zhang2025uabscan,p2:apisix2026_124680}---to
proof verification: the executor calls the verifier with the effective method
after override and the canonical path of the route it matched.
\emph{D-B} (view MAC, Algorithm~\ref{alg:db}) is, to our knowledge, the first
mechanism that cryptographically ties the view a PoP verifier accepted to the
operation the executor dispatches; unlike hop-level request
synchronisation~\cite{topcuoglu2025sync}, it binds to the client's proof rather
than to the previous hop. \emph{D-C} is a strict canonical edge
(\texttt{HTU\_CANON=strict} plus strict profiles).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{D-B: verifier emits, executor recomputes the view MAC
with $\mathsf{mac}(\pi,m,p)=\mathrm{HMAC}_K(\pi.\jti\,\|\,\mathrm{upper}(m)\,\|\,p\,\|\,\mathsf{th}(\pi.\mathit{jwk}))$, key $K$ shared by verifier and executor, $\|$ a newline.}\label{alg:db}
\SetKwProg{Fn}{}{:}{}
\Fn{\textsc{Emit}$(\pi, m_v, u_v)$ \Cmt*[f]{verifier, after Alg.~\ref{alg:verifier} allows}}{
  $\mu\gets\mathsf{mac}(\pi, m_v, \mathsf{canon\_path}(u_v))$\;
  return \hdr{X-Verified-View: v1;}$\mathrm{b64u}(\mu)$\;
}
\Fn{\textsc{Check}$(r)$ \Cmt*[f]{executor, after override and routing, before the handler}}{
  \lIf{not exactly one \hdr{X-Verified-View}}{reject \texttt{view\_mac\_missing}/\texttt{multiple}}
  $\mu\gets$ MAC carried in \hdr{X-Verified-View}\;
  parse $\pi$ from \hdr{DPoP} without verifying\Cmt*[f]{$\Ver$ already did}\;
  $m_x\gets$ effective method; $(\mathit{tmpl},\theta_x)\gets$ matched route\;
  \lIf{some $\theta_x[i]\in\{\texttt{.},\texttt{..}\}$}{reject \texttt{view\_param\_dot\_segment}}
  $p_x\gets\mathsf{canon\_path}(\mathsf{substitute}(\mathit{tmpl},\mathsf{pctenc}(\theta_x)))$\;
  $\mu'\gets\mathsf{mac}(\pi, m_x, p_x)$\;
  \lIf{$\neg\mathsf{ConstEq}(\mu,\mu')$}{reject 401 \texttt{view\_mac\_mismatch}}
  run handler\;
}
\end{algorithm}

$\mathsf{canon\_path}$ is the RFC~3986-normalised path only: query and fragment
dropped, unreserved escapes decoded, hex upper-cased, dot segments removed;
\texttt{\%2F} is not decoded and \texttt{//} is not merged. Swapping the proof
or key changes \jti or \jkt and breaks the MAC; a forged header fails without
$K$; a duplicate is rejected. The gateway must still strip client-supplied
\hdr{X-Verified-*} at ingress for availability and for the headers the MAC does
not cover---which Envoy, Kong and HAProxy do not do by default (P2). D-B runs
on four frameworks; its implementations pass \NumDBUnitPy{} (Python),
\NumDBUnitNode{} (Node), \NumDBUnitJava{} (Java) and \NumDBUnitCs{} (C\#)
shared-vector tests.

\paragraph{Security (E7, E8).} We re-ran every confirmed \Aleak witness
(\NumDefWitnesses{} on \NumDefStacks{} gateway/framework stacks from the NGINX,
APISIX and Kong campaigns) and the \NumDefEFourTotal{} E4 violations under each
configuration, against baselines \emph{B-edge} (status quo), \emph{B-origin}
(raw target re-verified at the executor), \emph{B-strict} (strict profiles)
and \emph{B-optional} (\htm/\htu checks off). All \NumDefRepTopos{} topologies
gave identical results in two replications. Table~\ref{tab:defence} and
Figure~\ref{fig:defence}(a) give the residuals. D-A and D-B remove exactly the
method override and every \GB path split, and nothing else: all
\NumDefResidualDA{} query- and body-dispatch witnesses remain, because \htu
and the MAC cover the path only (\S\ref{sec:ablation} measures query and body
binding). Under \Aclient they differ. D-A asks the policy about the
\emph{routed} view and closes all \NumDefEFourTotal{} E4 violations without
losing a legitimate request. D-B binds execution to the verifier's
\emph{canonical} view while the PDP matched the raw path, so the \texttt{/../}
classes survive (\NumDefEFourDB{}): D-B restores \emph{binding} soundness but
not \emph{policy} soundness unless the policy also reads the canonical view.
D-C equals B-strict, because the strict gateways reject first; it closes
\texttt{\%2f} and dot segments but not \texttt{\#} or override. B-optional
reproduces every witness and, on the full corpus, raises the hits from
\NumBEdgeFastapi{} to \NumBOptFastapi{} (FastAPI) and from \NumBEdgeExpress{}
to \NumBOptExpress{} (Express): the \htm/\htu checks are what keep the path
mutations out.

\paragraph{Which steps matter (\Abl{2}).} Leaving one D-B canonicalisation
step out (replayed offline with D-B's own functions; \NumAtwoAgree{}/\NumAtwoAgree{}
agreement with the live runs) shows that \AblTwoResult{}. No step affects \GA
dispatch, the E4 dot-segment classes or the rewrite false rejections.

\paragraph{False rejection.} We sent \NumFRRRequests{} legitimate requests
behind NGINX in six scenarios---identity, strip-prefix, version rewrite,
trailing-slash strip, percent-encoded identifiers, and h2c-to-HTTP/1.1---each
with a valid proof for the public URL, counting a reject only where B-edge
served the request (Figure~\ref{fig:defence}b). Over the synthetic mix D-A
and D-B reject \FRRAllDAB{} (\FRRAllN{}, 95\% CI \FRRAllDABCI{}); the honest
reading is per scenario. Every rewritten request is rejected (\FRRRewriteN{}):
the proof binds the public URI, the executor sees the internal path. Without
rewriting, the rate is \FRRNonRewriteDAB{} (\FRRNonRewriteN{}), all from two
encoder seams we can fix: a catch-all parameter containing \texttt{/}, and
Kestrel keeping \texttt{\%2F} encoded. Stricter edge matching is no
substitute: it rejects every percent-encoded identifier (\FRRAllStrict{}
overall) and still passes \texttt{\#} and override. The rewrite failures are
not an implementation artefact but a statement of the problem: a path-only view
binding is sound only if the proof and the executor agree on \emph{whose} view
is meant, i.e.\ a gateway that rewrites must hand the executor its
public-to-internal path map or the verifier must MAC the path it forwards.
This is the executor-view contract of \S\ref{sec:discussion}.

\paragraph{Latency.} At \PerfRate{}\,req/s, the only load level without errors
(\PerfRuns{} runs $\times$ \PerfRunSecs{}\,s, k6, NGINX), D-B changes the
median by \DefenceOverheadPfifty{} and the p99 by
\DefenceOverheadPninetynine{} (Cliff's $\delta$ \DefenceCliffFastapi{} and
\DefenceCliffSpring{}, negligible; Figure~\ref{fig:latency}); the HMAC adds no
hop. D-A on FastAPI has a heavy tail (p95 \PerfDAFastapiPninetyfive{} vs.\
\PerfBEdgeFastapiPninetyfive{}\,ms, $\delta$ \PerfDACliffFastapi{}), which
we attribute to that executor's verifier call, since Spring D-A tracks B-edge.
From \PerfSatRate{}\,req/s every stack on our shared laptop saturated with
NGINX 502s (no 401s) that did not reproduce between runs, so we rank nothing
above \PerfRate{}\,req/s.
