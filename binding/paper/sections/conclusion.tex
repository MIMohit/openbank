\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A sender-constrained proof binds what the verifier sees, not what the
executor runs. In layered deployments those differ by design, because the
query, the body and method overrides are not covered, and by interpretation,
because gateways and frameworks read the same path differently. Across
\NumTopologies{} topologies a single leaked proof selected more than one
operation in \NumVulnTopologies{}, and under default configurations
interpretation differentials alone turned a \texttt{deleteAccount} proof into
\texttt{deleteStatement}. A legitimate client with a valid proof crossed tenants
or reached admin routes past path policies in \NumEFourClasses{} classes,
two of them past a strict allow-list. Re-checking the proof at the origin did not help.
Binding soundness has to be a property of the deployment: the
standard should name the executor's view, and gateways should authorize the
representation they forward. Checking the executor's routed view removed the override and path
differentials (with D-B at negligible median latency), but not query or body dispatch, and
rejected rewritten paths: the binding must be defined on the executor's view.
